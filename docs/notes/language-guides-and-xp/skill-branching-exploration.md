Status: discussion proposal, 2026-09-24. The user selected the budget of four

## What was wrong with the first sketch

The previous proposal mixed semantic topics (time, location), operations
(negation), and social purposes (asking). Some edges suggested increasing
complexity without identifying what actually became more demanding. Broad
family names also risked being mistaken for observable entry skills.

Keep a distinction between an unscored family heading and its entry descriptor.
There are four headings and 28 candidate descriptors, not 32 scored nodes.
A branch proposes an extension of an observable capability. It is neither a
logical subtype relation nor a validated acquisition prerequisite.

## Three ways to organize the same budget

| Basis | Four possible families | Benefit | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Familiar subject areas | People/things; space/time; action/change; social exchange | Easy browsing and concrete examples | Negation, modality, questions and reference recur under every topic; duplicates criteria and guides |
| Communicative purposes | Inform; inquire; influence; maintain relationships | Immediately meaningful conversational goals | The same grammatical capability occurs under many purposes; reported questions and indirect requests cross boundaries |
| Meaning being managed | Reference; situations; claims; discourse | Gives grammar evidence a primary home while allowing combinations | Less immediately familiar labels; time and discourse risk becoming overloaded |

The third is the candidate explored below because this system needs both grammar
guides and evidence matching. This is a product-design inference, not a claim
that linguistic research establishes these four roots. The second remains a
serious alternative if conversational goals become the main organizing purpose.
It could also supply task/example tags without creating another scored tree.

Croft distinguishes reference, predication and modification, and separates the
function of an expression from the semantic kind of thing it expresses. This
supports examining meaning/function rather than building an English word-class
tree; it does not supply the four families below. Only the publisher's chapter
extract and contents were reviewed. [@croft_propositional_acts2022]
ACTFL describes consistent performance across contexts, not one-off completion,
and explicitly says higher-level functions can be introduced earlier. Its
levels do not establish this proposed beginner/intermediate placement.
[@actfl_cando_structure2026]

## Candidate: four scopes of meaning

English examples below illustrate intended meanings, not obligatory English
constructions. No article, copula, inflection, pronoun, word order or explicit
connector is required universally. Each label still needs a precise criterion,
positive examples and near misses before adoption.

| Family / entry descriptor | Beginner branch | Intermediate extension 1 | Intermediate extension 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reference: identify a referent | Specify a referent | Identify by relationships | Identify by a description |
| (same entry) | Manage shared reference | Track competing referents | Distinguish members and sets |
| Situations: express a situation | Structure a situation | Change participant focus | Represent caused change |
| (same entry) | Locate a situation | Relate spatial frames | Relate times and phases |
| Claims: affirm or negate | Bound a claim | Control scope | Establish a comparison |
| (same entry) | Express nonactual meaning | Distinguish modal force | Reason within a possibility |
| Discourse: make a relevant contribution | Connect contributions | Explain or counter expectations | Attribute a perspective |
| (same entry) | Guide an exchange | Resolve an information gap | Negotiate a joint action |

Reference: identifying something is extended by specifying which referent, or
managing its availability as discourse continues. Beginner specification uses
simple descriptions; intermediate identification combines a relationship chain
or a describing situation. Shared reference develops into tracking competing
referents and distinguishing a set from its members.

Situations: basic states/events gain participant structure or a setting.
Participant structure extends to changed focus and caused change; setting extends
to spatial frames and temporal relationships. Basic property description remains
at entry; comparison adds an explicit standard in Claims.

Claims: a basic affirmation/denial gains bounds or nonactual interpretation.
Bounds extend to operator scope and comparison thresholds. Nonactual meanings
extend to modal distinctions and reasoning under assumptions. This family has a
less direct parent-child relation than Reference: affirmation does not encompass
all modality, and must not be mistaken for a prerequisite.

Discourse: a relevant contribution extends toward relating contributions or
guiding an exchange. The first develops inferential links and source attribution;
the second develops repair and negotiated action. A greeting can be evidence for
the entry descriptor; it is not evidence for all discourse capabilities.

## Boundary tests

| Meaning/example | Primary distinction | Additional direct evidence, if present |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The red cup | Reference: select the intended object | No independent assertion that a cup is red |
| The cup is red | Situations: attribute a property | Reference to the cup |
| Two of those cups | Reference: identify a subset | Claims: quantity bounding, if separately evidenced |
| Not all cups are clean | Claims: negation scope | Reference to a set and description of a state |
| Ana made Bea leave | Situations: caused change | Participant reference |
| Bea left because Ana asked | Discourse: reason linking contributions | Event roles and time, when expressed |
| She may leave | Claims: modal meaning | Context is needed to distinguish permission from possibility |
| Can you open it? | Discourse: request, when context supports it | Do not automatically award ability merely for interrogative form |
| She said he would come | Discourse: attribute a perspective | Reference tracking and relative time |
| If it rains, we will stay | Claims: a hypothetical relation | Event-time evidence; no separate conditional clone under Time |

These are manual design probes, not validated Jev outputs. Each evidence decision
needs source spans and relevant context, including preceding contributions where
required. Polite intent or successful reference may be indeterminate from a
single isolated sentence. Score uncertainty rather than fabricating certainty.

## Current catalog coverage and unresolved costs

This is a family-level coverage check, **not a completed 45-to-28 crosswalk**.

- Reference group mostly maps to Reference. Quantity crosses reference-to-sets
  and bounding claims; possession is a simple relational reference, not inherently
  intermediate. The intermediate criterion would concern combined relationships.
- Properties map to basic situation description and claim comparison/degree.
  A clear home for qualified_property requires its criterion to be reviewed.
- Events map to situation structure, participant focus and caused changes.
  Transfer remains a participant relation; complex transfer needs explicit coverage.
- Space maps to situation setting and spatial frames, including motion/path.
- Time is compressed into Locate a situation and Relate times and phases. Past,
  future, sequence and aspect cannot be treated as demonstrated together.
- Operators map chiefly to Claims; questions map to guiding exchanges. Embedded
  questions may combine reference, reported perspective and information-seeking.
- Connections map to conditional reasoning, reason/expectation links and source
  attribution. Nested reports require more precise criteria than the label provides.
- Social greetings/check-ins/courtesy map to relevant contributions and exchange
  management. Negotiation and repair introduce requirements not separately named
  in the current catalog and are proposed additions.

The hardest open problems:

1. Time and aspect could be too much for one intermediate node. If we cannot write
   a cohesive criterion, exchange a slot elsewhere rather than hide many skills
   in unbounded subcriteria or increase the selected 28-node budget.
2. Discourse combines relations between meanings with interpersonal coordination.
   Compare the communicative-purpose alternative before accepting this boundary.
3. Several intermediate functions have simple beginner realizations. The actual
   challenge must be contextual control, not the first appearance of a keyword.
4. A more abstract label can conceal the original maintenance cost. Count guide
   sections and evaluation contrasts, not just visible nodes.
5. Family containment and developmental extension are different relations. A
   single tree cannot prove both. The proposed edges express extension only.

## Graph and evidence rules

Keep one primary parent per node for the 4/8/16 presentation. Add only selected,
meaningful combination edges; do not generate nodes for all combinations.
For example, managing reference can contribute to attributing a perspective;
locating a situation can contribute to reasoning within a possibility.
These example edges run beginner-to-intermediate and preserve acyclicity.

Do not award XP at each ancestor automatically. A descendant observation may
contribute to a displayed family summary without becoming several independent
reward events. Multiple directly evidenced skills are possible in one utterance;
allocation of reward remains a separate, undecided policy. Unobserved is unknown.

Do not require roots to be completed before branches can collect evidence.
Product stage labels are provisional complexity descriptions, not CEFR/ACTFL
certificates or a universal language-independent acquisition order.
