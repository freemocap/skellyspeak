## Learner guide

Locate events in time and express their temporal structure.

### Present situations

Situate a state, activity or habit in the current reference period.

Present-tense forms can describe current situations or habits. The surrounding exchange tells you which meaning applies.

> Trabajo aquí.

I work here.

The present describes the speaker's current work situation.

### Past events

Locate an event before now or another established reference time.

Use a past construction that matches what you mean. Fui can describe a completed trip; iba can describe an ongoing or habitual past situation. A short answer can inherit its time frame from the question.

> Ayer fui al mercado.

Yesterday I went to the market.

Fui with ayer expresses a completed earlier trip; an unrelated article mistake would not change that temporal construction.

### Future events

Locate an event after now or another established reference time.

Ir a followed by an infinitive is one way to talk about a planned future action. Future forms and contextually future present forms are also possible.

> Mañana voy a cocinar.

Tomorrow I am going to cook.

Mañana and voy a cocinar establish future reference.

### Duration and frequency

Express how long or how often situations occur.

Use time phrases to state how long or how often something happens.

> Estudio español todos los días.

I study Spanish every day.

Todos los días states the frequency.

### Sequence

Order events relative to one another.

Primero, después and other linking expressions can place events in order without claiming that one caused another.

> Primero cenamos y después salimos.

First we had dinner and then we went out.

The two events have an explicit order; context supplies their past reading.

### Ongoing and completed events

Distinguish unfolding, bounded, completed or changing phases of an event.

Estar with a gerund can describe an activity in progress. Completed actions use other constructions appropriate to the time frame.

> Estoy leyendo.

I am reading.

Estoy plus leyendo presents the activity as ongoing.

### Time and events

Locate events in time and express their temporal structure.

Boundary: One correctly expressed temporal relationship is sufficient. Present properties alone do not establish temporal skill. Judge meaning in context, not a tense label.

Judge the time relationship the learner actually expresses. Spanish uses present forms for current situations and habits; preterite for bounded past events; imperfect for past background or habits; and ir a + infinitive, future forms, or contextually future present forms for later events. Historical present can narrate past events when context supports it. Estar + gerund expresses an ongoing activity; ya + an appropriate completed construction can mark completion. Ayer fui al mercado correctly locates a completed event. Ayer yo ir al mercado is an unsuccessful finite past construction, not a correct past use. Ayer alone can be a correct contextual answer to ¿Cuándo fuiste? Missing an article elsewhere does not defeat a correctly formed temporal construction. Sequence does not imply cause. A hypothetical or polite past-form expression is not necessarily past reference.

These core examples are usable in Spain. Accept valid regional uses rather than requiring one national norm. Both simple and compound past constructions may be appropriate depending on context; do not require a compound past simply because an event happened today.
