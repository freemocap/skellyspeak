## Learner guide

Request, propose and negotiate shared or individual action.

### Requests

Ask another participant to do or stop doing something.

Ask for an action directly or with a conventional polite question. Poder in a request is not necessarily a question about physical ability.

> ¿Puedes cerrar la puerta, por favor?

Can you close the door, please?

The expected response is closing the door.

### Offers and invitations

Propose one's help or another participant's involvement.

Offer your help or invite the other person to join an activity.

> ¿Quieres venir a cenar?

Would you like to come for dinner?

In this setting the question invites participation.

### Suggestions

Put forward a possible course of action for consideration.

Podemos followed by an infinitive can propose something to do together.

> Podemos ir mañana.

We could go tomorrow.

In a planning exchange, this proposes an option.

### Commitments

Undertake responsibility for a future action.

Use wording that undertakes responsibility when making a commitment; future time alone is not a promise.

> Te prometo que voy a llamarte.

I promise I will call you.

Prometo makes the undertaking explicit.

### Accepting and declining

Respond to a request, offer, invitation or proposed action.

A short acceptance or refusal depends on the proposal it answers. You can decline politely without a long explanation.

> Lo siento, no puedo.

Sorry, I cannot.

Following an invitation, this declines it.

### Negotiating a plan

Adjust proposed action to reconcile participants' needs or constraints.

Respond to a proposed plan with an alternative that addresses the obstacle.

> A las seis no puedo; ¿quedamos a las siete?

I cannot make six; shall we meet at seven?

The speaker changes the proposed time to resolve a constraint.

### Coordinating action

Request, propose and negotiate shared or individual action.

Boundary: Require a successful request, proposal, commitment or response about action. Merely describing a desire or future event is insufficient.

Language: Spanish
Variety: Spain

Spanish can request action with an imperative or a conventional question such as ¿Puedes repetirlo?; do not require an imperative.
