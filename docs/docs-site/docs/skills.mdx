# Explore language skills

Open **Progress → Skills** to see eight conversation skills for the selected language. Select **How skills work** to reopen the in-app introduction.

<UserDemo view="skills" />

## What the skills describe

Skills group things you do in conversation: exchange information, describe people and places, talk about time, express feelings and viewpoints, coordinate action, explain reasons and connections, discuss possibilities and constraints, and manage the conversation itself.

A reply can provide evidence for several skills. Small mistakes elsewhere in the reply do not automatically prevent a correctly demonstrated skill from counting.

## Read points and levels

The app assigns skill points when its assessment identifies qualifying evidence. Skill levels use increasing point thresholds: level 1 starts at 1 point, level 2 at 2, level 3 at 3, level 4 at 5 and level 5 at 8.

Your overall language skill level is your **weakest skill's level**. The chart's gold ring marks the next level; **To reach level** identifies skills that have not reached it yet. Increasing just the strongest skill will not raise the overall level while a weaker one remains below the threshold.

These are practice levels within the app, not CEFR levels or test results.

## Use a skill guide

1. Select a skill in the Skills view.
2. Read its guide and examples. Examples illustrate the skill in the selected language and variety.
3. Inspect your own replies that counted as evidence for it.
4. Choose **Use this in a … conversation** to start a conversation focused on that skill.
5. Use **All … skills** to return to the overview.

For repeated phrase work, [Practice](./practice) also offers skill selection when adding cards.

## Inspect the evidence

Read the quoted source and the explanation before treating a point as meaningful. Evidence can include information about the attempt, assessment provenance and in-app assistance. Outside assistance may be unknown.

If an assessment should not contribute to progress, use **Exclude assessment from progress** on its record. The record remains available for inspection. **Excluded · restore assessment** reverses that choice. Exclusion is not the same as deleting a conversation or editing its text.

Use the [XP report](./progress) to inspect the numerical contribution of skills across conversations, and [Coach](./coach) for help with a particular message.
