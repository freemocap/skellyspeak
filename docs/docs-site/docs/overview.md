# Start using SkellySpeak

SkellySpeak is a language practice app with three main places: **Chat** for conversations, **Practice** for repeating short phrases aloud, and **Progress** for inspecting your recorded activity and language skills.

These guides describe the current app. Interactive examples use sample data: they do not connect to your account, record your microphone, or save learning progress.

Your installed release may have different controls. Check **Settings → Updates**
for your app version and available updates if a step does not match your screen.

## Set up the app

1. [Download and install SkellySpeak](/download).
2. On the language screen, choose the languages you want to practise and the language to **Start in**. Choose a variety when offered.
3. Set the language used for explanations and the interface language. These are separate choices from the language you are learning.
4. Continue to **AI access**. Sign in to the hosted service, or configure a self-hosted SkellySpeak server. The account display shows your current allowance and usage.
5. Test your microphone if you plan to speak. You can also type. **Set up later** lets you finish setup without a connection; generating conversations, feedback and speech still needs working AI access.

You can change these choices later in **Settings**. On a narrow screen, open **More → Settings**.

## Try a first conversation

Open Chat using the **SkellySpeak name or logo**. Open **Options** to choose the conversation difficulty and other starting preferences. Let the partner begin, choose a topic, or write your own first message.

Read the reply, tap a word for its meaning, and answer. Use **Suggest a reply** when you need a starting point; inserting a suggestion puts it in your draft so you can edit it before sending.

Follow [your first chat](./chat) for a complete walkthrough, including voice recording and reading help.

## Try speaking practice

Open **Practice**, add a practice card, listen to its reference, and record yourself saying it. Compare the transcript and recordings, then try again. [The Practice guide](./practice) explains cards, recording modes and comparison views.

## Inspect what was recorded

Open **Progress**. Its **Skills**, **XP** and **Effort** tabs answer different questions: which conversation skills have evidence, where points came from, and what activity you have completed. [Read the progress guide](./progress) before interpreting the numbers.
